01 Named after a car in traffic

A neighbour­hood lounge on Church Street.

Alto opened on Church Street because Caversham was short of a room that worked for a flat-white at ten, a long lunch at one and a martini at eight — without changing clothes. So we made one.

Mismatched chairs. Books no one finished. Mr Moose, our stuffed moose. Wallpaper your nan would either love or refuse to look at. It's the kind of place you forget the time in — which is good, because we're open until late.

"We like to keep things laid-back. Come for breakfast, stay for dinner, leave with a cocktail. Or just stay for the cocktail."

What people order twice.

A small handful of the menu — a kitchen note, really, on what we cook well and people remember.

i.

Duck Pancakes

Slow-cooked duck, hot pancakes, cucumber sticks, spring onion and a proper hoisin. The thing everyone steals off everyone's plate.

Tapas · all day
ii.

White Orchid

A tequila cocktail with a quiet floral lift — the one we'd point you towards if you said "surprise me, but make it pretty".

Bar · from 5pm
iii.

Mango & Passion Fruit Cheesecake

The dessert that gets ordered before the mains are cleared. Bright, sharp, exactly cold enough.

Pudding · all day
iv.

Build-your-own tapas

Four-fifty-plus possible combinations on paper. In practice, four small plates and a glass of something is usually the right answer.

After 3pm
v.

Brunch, the long version

Pancakes, eggs done four ways, halloumi, smashed avo, full plates and small ones — served from when the doors open until well after the morning rush.

9am — 2pm
02 Everyone's welcome — that's the whole point

A room that works for whoever walks in.

Dogs

Sleeping bulldogs welcome. Water bowls under the bench. Treats sometimes appear.

Kids

A proper kids' menu — not chicken nuggets as an afterthought. Crayons live by the door.

Vegan & GF

Dedicated menus, not asterisks. Coeliac-safe options the kitchen actually trains on.

Access

Step-free entrance, wheelchair-accessible loo, and seating we can rearrange to suit.
